Selected builds

Antiques Dealer, Toronto

Public catalogue + private upload tool

Problem: Inventory existed only in-store — no way to see what's in stock before visiting.

What I built: A searchable public catalogue with filters, photos and pricing, plus a simple private upload tool for new arrivals.

Why this approach: One core feature matched to the shop's daily rhythm — unpack new stock, get it online, nothing more complex.

What changed: Customers browse the full current inventory before ever walking in; listing new stock takes minutes, not a developer.

Custom Framing Studio

Guided multi-step quote form

Problem: Every quote request started as a bare "contact us" — size, style, mat and glazing all chased down manually.

What I built: A guided, multi-step quote form — photo upload, dimensions, frame style, mat, glazing, notes, contact details — in one submission.

Why this approach: Removes the back-and-forth entirely; every request that lands is already quotable.

Rare Book & Fine Art Dealer

Unified searchable catalogue, ~21,000 items

Problem: ~21,000 items split across two outdated websites, most without images, no way to search by keyword, category or price.

What I built: One unified, searchable catalogue with category and price-range filtering across the entire inventory.

Why this approach: Consolidation into a single link and search experience, instead of two disconnected sites.

What changed: The whole inventory now lives in one place — searchable and filterable — instead of split across two disjointed systems.
